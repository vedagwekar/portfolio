the idea i took from this book is that you don't become yourself by adding more. you become yourself by carrying things, then dropping them, then starting over like it's play.

## what it says

zarathustra comes down from a mountain after ten years alone to teach people about the overman, someone who makes their own values instead of inheriting them. almost nobody listens. most of the book is him wandering, giving speeches, getting frustrated, and going back up the mountain to think.

the part that stuck with me most is near the start, the three metamorphoses. the spirit first becomes a camel, which kneels down and takes on as much weight as it can: rules, duties, what it's told it should be. then it becomes a lion, which fights the dragon called "thou shalt" and learns to say no. then it becomes a child, which is a new beginning, a game, a yes.

> of three metamorphoses of the spirit do i tell you: how the spirit becometh a camel, the camel a lion, and the lion at last a child.

## where it collides with everything else

i read this the summer before moving from calgary to waterloo, so it hit at a weird time. i felt like a camel. i'd spent years taking on whatever was expected: grades, applications, being the person people thought i'd be.

- learning guitar feels like the three stages. first you just carry the chords and scales someone gives you. then you get annoyed with sounding like everyone else and stop doing what the tabs say. then, sometimes, you just play.
- basketball is like that too. you run the drills until they're automatic, and the best moments in a game are when you stop thinking about the drills at all.
- in code, the camel is copying patterns from documentation. the lion is the moment you understand why a pattern exists well enough to break it. the child is building something no one asked for because it's fun.

the eternal recurrence idea also stayed with me: if you had to live this exact day again, forever, would you be okay with that? it's a hard question to answer honestly about a normal tuesday.

## what i'm doing differently

going into first year, i want to notice which things i'm carrying because i chose them, and which ones i'm just carrying.
