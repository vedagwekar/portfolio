most people describe *the picture of dorian gray* as a book about vanity, and i agree, though the vanity in it goes further than wanting to stay young. what dorian actually gets is a life where nothing he does leaves a mark on him, and by the end of the book i was convinced that a person like that has stopped being a person.

the bargain works like this. basil paints him, dorian wishes the painting would age instead of him, and it does. the cruelty goes onto the canvas and his face stays twenty. i used to think of a self as something you have, but reading this i started to think it's closer to a record of what you've done, kept in your body and in how people treat you afterward. dorian moves his record into a locked room upstairs. he keeps acting for eighteen years and never becomes anyone new, because nothing he does reaches him.

lord henry bothered me more than dorian did. he never does anything himself. he says clever things at dinner and watches dorian live them out. wilde gives him a line early on that describes exactly what he's doing:

> to influence a person is to give him one's own soul. he does not think his natural thoughts, or burn with his natural passions.

henry hands over a philosophy and keeps none of the consequences. dorian takes the consequences and hides them. between the two of them, no one ever has to stand in front of what was done, and that arrangement is what wilde seems most afraid of.

when i tried to find where this shows up in my own life, i kept coming back to how much effort i put into how i look to other people. it's rarely anything big. it's picking what to say so i come across a certain way, or only mentioning the parts of a week that went well. none of it is lying, but it's a small version of what dorian does. the parts i'd rather not show go somewhere nobody looks, and usually that includes me.

tiktok is where i notice it most. i can scroll for an hour and come away with nothing i remember, and the whole time i'm not thinking about anything i actually did that day. everyone in the feed is showing their face and keeping the painting somewhere else, and after enough of it i start measuring my life against those faces. the two things feed each other. watching people keep up appearances makes me want to keep up mine, and the scrolling fills the time i might have spent looking at what i've done.

the vanity i recognize in myself is mostly a habit of avoiding the room upstairs. self reflection is the act of going up there, looking at the canvas, including the parts that got worse, and letting them change me. dorian never does it, and the longer he waits, the harder the door is to open.

the ending is what stays with me. dorian decides to destroy the portrait and stabs it. the servants come in to find the painting back as it was, and an old, ruined man on the floor they can only identify by his rings. he went after the evidence and it turned out the evidence was him.

the question i came away with is where my portrait is, and how long it's been since i went upstairs to look at it.
